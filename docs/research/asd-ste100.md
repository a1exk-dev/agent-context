# ASD-STE100: requirements and redistribution

Research for issue #4, under map #2 (the `agent-instructions` skill spec). Sources were checked on 2026-10-01. This note paraphrases the standard. It quotes only short phrases. It is not legal advice.

## Answer

- The current issue is **ASD-STE100 Issue 9, dated 2025-01-15**. Issue 9 changed its status from a specification to a standard. Issue 10 is planned for January 2028. [1][2][3]
- The standard has two parts. Part 1 has **53 writing rules in 9 sections**. Part 2 is a dictionary with **875 approved words and 1274 words that are not approved**. [2][4]
- The key numeric limits are:
  - 20 words for each sentence in procedures. [4 §5]
  - 25 words for each sentence in descriptive text. [4 §6]
  - 6 sentences for each paragraph. [4 §6]
  - 3 words for each multi-word noun. [4 §2]
- STE has one official AI document: the STEMG white paper **"ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English and Artificial Intelligence (AI)"**, dated June 2026. It sets policy only. It adds no rules. [5]
- **Redistribution:** the standard is free to download, but you may not redistribute it. ASD keeps all rights and needs written authority for any reproduction, "in whole or in part". Eight listed groups of organizations get a free licence. An open-source skill repository is not one of them. [4 copyright notice]
- **Result for this repository:** do not embed the dictionary or the rule text. Paraphrase the rules, link to the official free download, and let users supply their own copy for word-level checks.

## Writing rules (Part 1)

Section 4 has the general rules for sentences. Sections 5 and 6 have different rules for the two types of text. Section 5 covers procedures, which give instructions. Section 6 covers descriptive text, which gives information. [4 §4]

The rules in each section:

- **§1 Words (1.1–1.14).**
  - Use only these words: approved dictionary words, technical nouns, and technical verbs.
  - Use each approved word only as its listed part of speech and only with its approved meaning.
  - Use only the approved forms of verbs and adjectives.
  - Technical nouns:
    - Use them only when they fit one of 22 categories, and only when your company or subject field approves them.
    - Do not use them as verbs. Do not use slang or jargon as a technical noun.
    - Use one technical noun for each item.
  - Technical verbs must fit one of 4 categories. Do not use them as nouns.
  - Use American English spelling, as given in Merriam-Webster.
- **§2 Multi-word nouns (2.1–2.2).** A multi-word noun has a maximum of 3 words. When a technical noun is longer, write it in full.
- **§3 Verbs (3.1–3.7).**
  - Use only these verb forms:
    - infinitive
    - imperative
    - simple present
    - simple past
    - simple future
    - past participle, only as an adjective
  - Do not use auxiliary verbs to make complex verb constructions.
  - You can use the "-ing" form only in a technical noun.
  - Use the active voice. You can use the passive voice only in descriptive text, and only when the agent is unknown.
  - Use a verb to show an action, not a noun.
- **§4 Sentences (4.1–4.5).**
  - Write short, clear sentences.
  - Do not omit words, and do not use contractions.
  - Use vertical lists for complex text.
  - Use connecting words between related sentences.
  - Use articles and demonstrative adjectives before nouns. Rule 4.5 is new in Issue 9.
- **§5 Procedural writing (5.1–5.5).**
  - Use a maximum of **20 words** in each sentence.
  - Give one instruction in each sentence, unless the actions occur at the same time.
  - Use the imperative.
  - When the reader must know a condition first, put it at the start of the sentence, followed by a comma.
  - Notes give information, not instructions.
- **§6 Descriptive writing (6.1–6.6).**
  - Give information gradually.
  - Use key words to give the text a structure.
  - Use a maximum of **25 words** in each sentence.
  - Give each paragraph one topic and a maximum of **6 sentences**.
  - Do not use the imperative.
- **§7 Safety instructions (7.1–7.3).**
  - Use a signal word for the level of risk ("warning" or "caution").
  - Start with a clear command or condition.
  - Then explain the risk.
- **§8 Punctuation and word count (8.1–8.7).**
  - Do not use the semicolon.
  - Use hyphens only between words that are directly related. Rule 8.3 lists the permitted uses of parentheses.
  - Word count:
    - In a vertical list, a colon ends a sentence.
    - Text in parentheses counts as 1 word.
    - Numbers, units, abbreviations, identifiers, quoted text, titles, labels, and proper nouns each count as 1 word.
    - A hyphenated word counts as 1 word.
- **§9 Writing practices (9.1–9.4 and GR-1 to GR-8).**
  - When a direct word replacement is not sufficient, change the sentence structure.
  - Use each approved word correctly.
  - Do not make phrasal verbs.
  - Keep the style consistent.
  - Section 9 also gives 8 general recommendations (GR) about these subjects:
    - "that" and "with"
    - pronouns and "this"
    - false friends
    - Latin abbreviations
    - inclusive language
    - possessives

STE was made for procedural and descriptive technical documentation, not for general writing. [3] A checker cannot verify all rules. One example: a checker cannot tell whether the first sentence of a paragraph is its topic sentence. [6]

## Dictionary and approved meanings (Part 2)

Each dictionary entry has four columns: the word and its part of speech, the approved meaning or the approved alternatives, an STE example, and a non-STE example. [4 Part 2 intro]

- An UPPERCASE word is approved. A lowercase word is not approved, and its entry gives approved alternatives. Technical nouns and verbs given as alternatives have the marks (TN) and (TV).
- In general, each approved word has **one meaning and one part of speech**. Examples:
  - "check" is approved only as a noun.
  - "fall" means only to move down by gravity, not to decrease.
  - "start" replaces "begin", "commence", and "initiate".
  [3]
- The only approved modal verbs are CAN, MUST, and WILL:
  - Use CAN for possibility or ability. CAN replaces "may" and "would". COULD is permitted only as the past form of CAN, not to show possibility. "Might" has no entry, so it is not approved.
  - Use MUST for obligation. MUST replaces "should".
  - Use WILL for the simple future.
  - "ensure" becomes MAKE SURE.
  [4 Part 2]
- **Important for this map:** AGENT (n) is approved only with the meaning of a material made for a specified task (for example, a cleaning agent). Thus, "agent" in the sense of an AI agent is not the approved meaning. This repository must declare it as a technical noun. Other terms of this repository must also be technical nouns, for example "skill" and "rule". [4 Part 2, AGENT]
- The dictionary does **not** list technical nouns or technical verbs. [4 Part 2 intro]

## Technical nouns and technical verbs: how a project adds terms

- A project can use a term that is not in the dictionary when the term fits a category of technical nouns (rule 1.5, 22 categories) or technical verbs (rule 1.12, 4 categories). The category lists give examples only. They are not complete. [4 §1]
- **Technical noun category 19** covers computer science and information technology. Its examples include these terms: AI, artificial intelligence, chatbot, large language model, prompt engineering, token, embedding, hallucination, plug-in, metadata, file, database. [4 rule 1.5]
- **Technical verb category 2** covers computer processes. Its examples include these verbs: click, enter, type, open, save, delete, copy, paste, enable, disable, install, update, upload, download, debug, reboot. [4 rule 1.12]
- **How to add terms:**
  - Record the term in the company or project glossary or terminology database.
  - Make sure that the term is approved in your company or subject field (rule 1.8).
  - Make sure that the term is short and clear (rule 1.9).
  - Use only one term for each item (rule 1.11).
  [4 §1][3]
- If an approved verb gives the same instruction, use the approved verb, not a technical verb. [4 rule 1.12]
- A word that is not approved becomes acceptable only when it is part of a technical noun or verb. Keep this use to a minimum. [3]
- To add **dictionary words or meanings**, submit an STEMG Change Form. An accepted change comes into force only in a future issue, so you cannot use the change before then. [3]
- The STEMG tools page recommends this: set up a procedure to validate terms before you add them to the database, and give one person the task of database maintenance. [6]

## STE and Artificial Intelligence

There is one official document: the **STEMG white paper "ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English and Artificial Intelligence (AI)"**. The STEMG and its AI Task Team (AITT) wrote it in June 2026. [5] It has 3 pages and gives policy only:

- The standard comes first. AI must support STE requirements and must not override them.
- AI can help a human author. It cannot replace the human author. The human author or the organization stays responsible.
- Show where you used AI to help write content.
- Define internal policies for data handling, model validation, and quality assurance.
- The STEMG extends its non-endorsement policy to AI tools.

The Downloads page gives a short summary of the paper's warning: text that seems correct is not the same as verified compliance. [6]

Two more facts are related to AI:

- Issue 9 itself says that STE makes translation easier for "Large Language Models (LLM)". [4 general intro]
- Issue 9 lists AI terms as examples of technical nouns (category 19, above).

There is no STEMG material about writing instructions *for* AI agents. The use of STE for agent instructions is a community practice. It is not an ASD position.

## Machine-readable dictionaries and open-source checkers

ASD publishes no official machine-readable dictionary and no API. Electronic look-up tools exist, but the official PDF is the reference. [6] A search on GitHub on 2026-10-01 found many community projects. Some important examples are in the table that follows. A code licence does not give rights to ASD content.

| Project | Licence | What it ships | Dictionary content |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [dfch/biz.dfch.AsdSte100Vocab](https://github.com/dfch/biz.dfch.AsdSte100Vocab) (+ Lookup, Rules, Mcp, Parser) | AGPL-3.0 / GPL-3.0 | Python library, MCP server, grammar | **Embeds the full Issue 9 dictionary as JSONL, with examples.** The licence status is not clear, because the README credits ASD copyright and says it is not affiliated with ASD. |
| [kibook/ste-dic](https://github.com/kibook/ste-dic) | none stated | Hunspell `.aff`/`.dic` spell-check files | Word lists. No licence. |
| [AminBlg/SimpleEnglish](https://github.com/AminBlg/SimpleEnglish) | MIT | Agent skill + linter | Ships no dictionary. It has an extractor that builds word lists from the user's own copy of the PDF. |
| [stazelabs/ste](https://github.com/stazelabs/ste) | MIT | Go linter | Builds its dictionary index from the user's own copy. Has an allow list for technical names. |
| [danyuchn/asd-ste100-skill](https://github.com/danyuchn/asd-ste100-skill) | MIT | Claude Code skill for agent-facing English | Ships no dictionary, because of the copyright notice. Dictionary rules are advisory only. |
| [amoslives/vale-ste](https://github.com/amoslives/vale-ste) | MIT | Vale style (regular-expression rules) | Mechanical rules only, with no dictionary |
| [mikkovihonen/stelint](https://github.com/mikkovihonen/stelint), [swarooppatilx/ste100](https://github.com/swarooppatilx/ste100), [osolmaz/ste100](https://github.com/osolmaz/ste100), [jyooi/agent-simple-english](https://github.com/jyooi/agent-simple-english) | MIT | Rule checkers | Not audited |
| [openste/openste](https://github.com/openste/openste) | MIT | "OpenSTE" community word set | An independent word set. It is not ASD-STE100. |

The pattern that is safe and most common: ship mechanical checks for the rules that a tool can count, and ship no dictionary. The mechanical checks include:

- sentence length
- semicolons
- passive voice
- "-ing" forms
- modal verbs that are not approved
- multi-word nouns with more than 3 words

For word-level checks, load the dictionary from the user's own copy of the PDF. Keep the project's technical nouns and verbs in a glossary that the project owns.

## Open points for the map

- The skill cannot embed the dictionary. Do we use the bring-your-own-PDF method for word checks, or the mechanical rules plus a project glossary only?
- The terms of this repository need a technical-noun glossary. Examples: "agent", "skill", "rule", "category", and the other `CONTEXT.md` terms. It is not yet decided where this glossary lives.
- A strict STE check of agent instructions needs a change to the repository's own style. Agent instructions often use "should" and "may", and they often use descriptive sentences in procedures. STE does not permit these.
- Do we reuse an existing MIT checker, such as Vale rules or stazelabs/ste, or do we write our own checks in `scripts/check.sh`?
